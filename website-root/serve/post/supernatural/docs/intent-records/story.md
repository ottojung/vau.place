title: Seven-case dossier structure
date: 2026/09/23
source: @ottojung
kind: requirement

The work is a dossier-like collection with a prologue, exactly seven principal case files, and a coda. The seven current cases are the Schaerbeek bit, the Heisenbug, Maxwell's Demon, the Leprechaun of Off-by-One, Mercury in Retrograde, the Crocodile in Vienna, and the deliberately natural boring crash. Small supporting anecdotes such as the Mark II moth may appear inside a case without becoming additional principal case files.

The investigator-narrator has, through accumulated experience, come to take supernatural explanations seriously. He is not merely pretending to believe for comic effect. He does not comfortably announce that belief because he expects disbelief from others and is somewhat reluctant to admit the extent of it even to himself.

title: Skeptical surface for skeptical readers
date: 2026/09/23
source: @ottojung
kind: requirement

The narrator presents cases with the habits and vocabulary of a skeptic: operational detail, alternative explanations, procedural reconstruction, evidence, qualifications, and attention to what can actually be established. The prose should tempt skeptical readers toward the supernatural interpretation without simply commanding them to accept it. The supernatural conclusion is normally hinted, invited, or treated as a possibility whose weight keeps increasing rather than declared as authorial fact.

title: Escalating loss of ordinary explanations
date: 2026/09/23
source: @ottojung
kind: requirement

Across the principal case files, the collection should broadly progress from sane and readily deniable strangeness toward increasingly extravagant, superstitious, or reality-bending interpretations. The escalation is part of the narrator's arc: his method remains meticulous while what he is willing to entertain becomes less ordinary. The natural boring crash is an intentional late exception and baseline rather than a failure of this pattern.

title: Schaerbeek is the sane opening case
date: 2026/09/23
source: @ottojung
kind: requirement

The Schaerbeek bit is the least insane principal case. It begins from a real, documented anomaly that admits a conventional technical explanation and should remain credible to an ordinary skeptical reader. The fictional framing and narrator's attention may hint that something stranger presses at the edges, but the case must not require a supernatural event to make sense.

$id-0105305593640677
title: Heisenbug behaves as if observation matters
date: 2026/09/23
source: @ottojung
kind: requirement

The Heisenbug case treats a difficult, preferably real or plausibly reconstructed debugging incident as if observation itself changes the phenomenon. Instrumentation, tracing, logging, or attempts to catch the bug should make it recede or alter its behavior. The story should leave open the technically ordinary explanations while making the narrator increasingly willing to describe the bug as something that does not want to be watched.

title: Maxwell's Demon couples a dream warning to an improbable event
date: 2026/09/23
source: @ottojung
kind: requirement

The Maxwell's Demon case centers on a genuinely extraordinary but technically possible event, ideally grounded in a real-world example of extreme improbability such as a meaningful hash collision or another similarly startling computational coincidence. Before the event, a programmer falls asleep and dreams of a large terrifying demon that tells him that the codebase or server room is cursed. The later improbable event gives the warning retrospective force without turning the story into a simple proof that the demon was objectively real.

The Mercury in Retrograde case documents a distinctive software failure whose operational timeline coincides disturbingly well with Mercury being in retrograde. It should provide enough specificity that causal inference becomes tempting while never relying on the narrator merely asserting astrology as the cause. title: Crocodile case escalates through misplaced causal seriousness
date: 2026/09/23
source: @ottojung
kind: requirement

The Crocodile in Vienna case concerns a crocodile appearing in Vienna, causing real local commotion while American software systems continue to operate normally. The narrator should treat the absence of transatlantic software effects with enough investigative seriousness that the case advances the collection's growing absurdity. The joke remains implicit: the geographical non-causation is obvious to the reader but is handled as evidence by a narrator whose standards of relevant causality have drifted.

title: Natural crash is the explicit mundane baseline
date: 2026/09/23
source: @ottojung
kind: requirement

One principal case is a genuinely ordinary server crash caused by a mundane environmental or operational mechanism such as heat or power failure. It is not secretly supernatural. Its purpose is to break the escalation pattern, give the reader a baseline, and show that the narrator is still capable of accepting a boring explanation when the evidence supports one.

The progression between cases should be visible not only in the alleged phenomena but in the narrator's reasoning. Early cases permit him to remain almost entirely inside conventional engineering explanation. Later cases show him spending more effort preserving skeptical form while entertaining premises that an earlier version of himself would have rejected.

title: Horror and humor emerge from serious procedure
date: 2026/09/23
source: @ottojung
kind: requirement

The collection's horror, uncanniness, and humor should usually arise from meticulous procedural seriousness applied to events whose implications are increasingly strange. Avoid announcing jokes, explaining absurdity after the reader can already see it, or replacing procedural detail with generic horror decoration. The narrator's composure and exactness should make the strange material more effective, not less.
