date: 2026/09/23

The work is a dossier-like collection with a prologue, exactly seven principal case files, and a coda. The seven current cases are the Schaerbeek bit, the Heisenbug, Maxwell's Demon, the Leprechaun of Off-by-One, Mercury in Retrograde, the Crocodile in Vienna, and the deliberately natural boring crash. The investigator-narrator starts as a disciplined skeptic. Through the cases he witnesses or learns of beings and phenomena that transform his understanding; by the later cases he believes supernatural beings are real within the fictional world, not merely an explanatory hypothesis. His remaining reticence concerns what his experience means, what the beings want, and what might happen next—not a duty to keep doubting that they exist. Show this change in his emotional responses, choices, relationships, rituals, fear, and the way he interprets unrelated events; avoid a confessional lecture.

title: Skepticism is established early, not required of every later case
date: 2026/10/13
source: @ottojung

The Schaerbeek bit and the Heisenbug establish the narrator's habits of evidence, procedural scrutiny, and rational skepticism. Later stories must NOT keep performing a compulsory skeptical rebuttal to beings the fiction has made real. In Maxwell's Demon and the Leprechaun, the supernatural beings genuinely exist within the story; the reader need not be offered a convenient mundane account or told the event remains unproven. The narrative may still distinguish actual sourced historical incidents from invention outside the fiction. Credibility acquired early permits increasingly direct, emotional, mystical, and frightening experience later.

title: Heisenbug behaves as if observation matters
date: 2026/09/23
source: @ottojung
kind: requirement

The Heisenbug case treats a difficult, preferably real or plausibly reconstructed debugging incident as if observation itself changes the phenomenon. Instrumentation, tracing, logging, or attempts to catch the bug should make it recede or alter its behavior. The story should leave open the technically ordinary explanations while making the narrator increasingly willing to describe the bug as something that does not want to be watched. The case must create genuine doubt, anxiety, and eventually fear rather than reading primarily as an ordinary debugging log decorated with jokes; its horror should emerge from the repeated observation-sensitive reversals and from the narrator's increasingly uneasy behavior while the procedural detail remains serious.

The Maxwell's Demon case is centrally about a real, frightening supernatural being within the fictional world. The Leprechaun of Off-by-One is fundamentally a folkloric tale about an actual small man in green clothes with a red beard who enters the server room and moves the loop bounds by one. His appearance, presence, manner, agency, and effect on those who encounter him carry the story; the computer anomaly is a consequence, not the subject of forensic rationalization. Mercury in Retrograde must be full mysticism, immersed in the language, lore, imagery and inner worldview of astrology—not a probabilistic correlation study of software failures. The last principal case is genuinely and demonstrably mundane: a server crashes from an ordinary environmental or operational failure, with a credible complete technical explanation.
