Wreck a nice beach, and the one clue that won't show up

ottojung.github.io/madgab · September 28, 2026

Mad Gab is a small party game about saying something and hearing something else. You say the clue out loud, the other players write down what they heard, and the joke lands when the two come apart in an interesting way. It's just a stupid game is the famous one. The canonical answer is Hits Justice Dupe Hid Came, which I have never once managed to hear on purpose.

There is a program called madgab that tries to do this mechanically. You give it a target phrase, it works out the phonemes, and then it searches an English dictionary for a different set of words covering the same sound stream with different word boundaries. Nothing about the pronunciation changes; only where you decide a word stops and the next one starts.

I did not write that program. Jack Danger started it in 2019 and there are 23 commits under his name in the history; there are 569 under mine. He built the thing, and my contributions were to keep going after the interesting part was already there. This post is a note about what that bought and what it did not.

What it says

Here is the current build on phrases I typed in myself. Every line is real output from the release binary, top result only.

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "hello world"
hell oh earl

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "the quick brown fox"
that week now knox

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "bring me a sandwich"
bar ing yes end which

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "you are beautiful"
y'are herb route a fell

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "taco cat"
tock oak at

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "I love you"
isle uhh view

hell oh earl is the one that made me realize this was worth the time. bar ing yes end which is bring me a sandwich with a completely different set of words and no obvious way to see it coming.

The one that matters for this post is not in that list, and getting it there was most of the work:

$ madgab --approximate --top 50 --pool-rank "recognize speech"
 9. [score 0.900, pool rank 9 of 18301] wreck a nice beach

wreck a nice beach for recognize speech. It is the ninth-best of 18,301 scored candidates, which is inside the top-50 bar the repository's own acceptance test uses. I did not put that phrase anywhere in the source; there is a whole test file, no_phrase_hard_coding.rs, whose only job is to fail the build if I ever did.

Why the easy version is hard

The difficulty is not where I expected it to be. Three things, roughly in the order I hit them.

Word boundaries move. The target recognize speech is /ɹˈɛkəɡnˌaɪzspˈitʃ/, and there is no stretch of that stream where wreck a nice is unambiguously wrong. A dictionary lookup at each position is not the question. The question is where to cut between neighbouring words, and those cuts are what you are actually searching over.

A locally bad word can be essential. dupe is a poor match for anything in It's just a stupid game on its own. It is only good in its specific slot, between its specific neighbours, inside the one phrasing that makes the whole thing work. Scoring that averages word quality across a clue will rank the right phrase for the wrong reasons and then refuse to reach it. Adding an explicit worst-word score axis, rather than only an aggregate, is what actually moved wreck a nice beach from pool rank 27 to rank 9.

And the interesting bit: the classical clue is not a vocabulary problem. This is the part I had wrong at the start and the part I am most glad to have measured. All five of its words are in the corpus, with pronunciations. The complete sequence is feasible in the fuzzy phonetic lattice and fits comfortably inside the normal edit budget. Removing the rarity bound and growing the vocabulary from about 50k words to about 281k does not make it appear. Nothing is missing. Bounded enumeration simply fails to construct a path that is sitting right there.

Which is the useful part. If the failure were a missing word, the fix would be a bigger dictionary. Because it is not, the fix cannot be a bigger beam either. It points at a different abstraction: searching over whole paths with a pronunciation DAG and k-best or A*-style guidance, instead of enumerating a Cartesian product of per-slot word lists and hoping the good corner is near the front.

What I did

Compactly, and mostly by extending what was already there rather than replacing it.

  • A WebAssembly build and browser front end, with generation in a Web Worker so the page stays responsive. It is deployed and you can use it: ottojung.github.io/madgab.
  • The approximate-search pipeline: an indel-capable fuzzy word matcher, a lattice over the target's phonemes, bounded per-span portfolios instead of an unbounded product, and final scoring on phonetic similarity, novelty, familiarity, rhythm and shape.
  • Regression tests and measurements, including the hard-coding fence above and a 24-target spread used to compare scoring variants on aggregate quality.
  • A long list of strategies tried and ruled out, each with a number attached: ordering keys, admission cuts, width and budget changes, depth caps, objective reweighting. Most measured negative. One landed, the worst-word axis above.

The classical case is documented rather than hard-coded: its acceptance test is marked #[ignore] with a pointer to the accepted-state write-up, so the limitation stays visible in the test output instead of being quietly papered over. A caveat I want to be plain about: a great deal of the experimentation was agent-assisted, and the repository's commit log is unusually candid about it. The judgement calls are still mine, but I could not have run that many priced experiments in a week otherwise.

Where it stands

The project is at an acceptable release state as of September 27 and the autonomous search work is paused. A single run takes about a second on my machine: roughly half to load the corpus, about a second to search.

The real progress: approximate mode works well, wreck a nice beach works, and a lot of general matching, scoring, runtime and diversity behaviour is measurably better than it was a week earlier.

The unresolved part, stated just as plainly: production search does not generate Hits Justice Dupe Hid Came. The closest things on offer are it justice too pad same and it justice too pad came — the right bones and the wrong middle. That is a real limit on a real puzzle, and I would rather say so than make the readme look better than the program is.
