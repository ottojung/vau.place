Wreck a nice beach, and the one clue that won't show up

ottojung.github.io/madgab · September 28, 2026

Mad Gab is a small party game about saying something and hearing something else. You say the clue out loud, the other players write down what they heard, and the joke lands when the two come apart in an interesting way. It's just a stupid game is the famous one. The canonical answer is Hits Justice Dupe Hid Came, which I am told is a favourite piece of party furniture, and which I have never once managed to hear on purpose.

There is a program called madgab that tries to do this mechanically. You give it a target phrase, it works out the phonemes, and then it searches an English dictionary for a different set of words that cover the same sound stream with different word boundaries. The word boundaries are the whole trick. Nothing about the pronunciation changes; only where you decide a word stops and the next one starts.

I did not write that program. Jack Danger wrote it, starting in 2019 in Ruby, rewriting it in Rust in May 2026, and adding the budgeted --approximate mode, the CLI, the CI, and the 140k-word IPA corpus that ships inside the binary. All of that is his, and the crate's own metadata still points at github.com/JackDanger/madgab. What I did was keep going after the interesting part had already been built, and this post is mostly an honest note about what that bought and what it did not.

What it says

Here is the current build, on phrases I typed in myself. Every line is real output from the release binary.

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "hello world"
hell oh earl

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "good morning"
go d'or ring

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "taco cat"
tock oak at

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "call me maybe"
corn team a. bee

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "the cat sat on the mat"
zach ass yeah tons m at

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "play games with me now"
plague aims a mean how

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "she sells sea shells"
see else if l.'s

$ madgab --approximate --top 1 "I love you"
isle uhh view

I like hell oh earl more than I expected to. I like zach ass yeah tons m at slightly less, which is fair, because the generator has no idea what it is doing beyond sound.

The one that matters for this post is not in that list, and getting it there was most of the work:

$ madgab --approximate --top 50 --pool-rank "recognize speech"
 9. [score 0.900, pool rank 9 of 18301] wreck a nice beach

wreck a nice beach for recognize speech. It is the ninth-best of 18,301 scored candidates, which is inside the top-50 bar the repository's own acceptance test uses, and it is not in the default top-10 display, because structure-diversity selection prefers some of its near neighbours. I did not put that phrase anywhere in the source. There is a whole test file, no_phrase_hard_coding.rs, whose only job is to fail the build if I ever did.

Why the easy version is hard

Approximate phonetic search is harder than it sounds, and the difficulty is not where I expected it to be. Four things, in the order I hit them.

Word boundaries move. The target recognize speech is /ɹˈɛkəɡnˌaɪzspˈitʃ/, and there is no stretch of that stream where wreck a nice is unambiguously wrong. A dictionary lookup at each position is not the question; the question is where to cut between neighbouring words, and those cuts are what you are actually searching over.

A locally mediocre word can be essential to a globally excellent phrase. This is the one that kept biting. dupe is not a good match for anything in It's just a stupid game on its own. It is only good in the specific slot, with the specific words on either side, inside the one phrasing that makes the whole thing work. Any scoring function that looks at a single word in isolation, or that averages word quality across the clue, will rank that phrase correctly for the wrong reasons and then refuse to reach it.

Naive widening explodes. The obvious fix is to admit more candidates per slot and let the search sort it out. The search cannot sort it out. The five slots needed for Hits Justice Dupe Hid Came sit at traversal indices 7, 0, 13, 99, 11, and the joint index space at that point is 160 × 7 × 160 × 160 × 93, which is 2,666,496,000 cells. To even admit the required tuple through a cheapest-first traversal needs a frontier of about 11,329 pops against a hard limit of 4,000. A variant that would actually reach it needs roughly 64,001 and gets OOM-killed. The distance is not 2×, it is three to five orders of magnitude, and no amount of tuning slides you across it.

And then the interesting bit: the classical clue is not a vocabulary problem. This is the part I had wrong at the start and the part I am most glad to have measured. All five target words are in the corpus. Their pronunciations are available. The complete sequence is feasible in the fuzzy phonetic lattice and fits comfortably inside the normal total edit budget. Removing the rarity bound and growing the vocabulary from about 50k words to about 281k does not make it appear. It is not missing a word. It is not missing a pronunciation. Bounded enumeration simply fails to construct a path that is sitting there.

Which is the useful part. If the failure were a missing word, the fix would be a bigger dictionary. Because it is not, the fix cannot be a bigger beam either. It points at a different abstraction: searching over whole paths with a real pronunciation DAG and k-best or A*-style guidance, or a strong backward suffix heuristic, rather than enumerating a Cartesian product of per-slot word lists and hoping the good corner is near the front.

What I did

Compactly, and mostly by extending what was already there rather than replacing it.

  • A WebAssembly build and browser front end, with generation in a Web Worker so the page stays responsive. It is deployed and you can use it: ottojung.github.io/madgab.
  • The approximate-search pipeline work: an indel-capable fuzzy word matcher, a lattice over the target's phonemes, a diverse beam that separates structural segmentation retention from lexical choice, bounded per-span portfolios instead of an unbounded product, and final scoring on phonetic similarity, novelty, familiarity, rhythm and shape.
  • Regression tests and measurements, including the hard-coding fence above and a 24-target spread used to compare scoring variants on aggregate quality.
  • A long list of strategies tried and ruled out, each with a number attached: per-slot ordering keys, per-slot admission cuts, width and budget changes, fill strategies, depth caps, shortlist widths, objective reweighting, adjacency substitutions. Most measured negative. One landed: adding a worst-word score axis, which moved wreck a nice beach from pool rank 27 to rank 9 and raised the share of all-content-word proposals in the spread from 0.104 to 0.185, at the price of some list diversity.

The classical case is documented rather than hard-coded. Its acceptance test is marked #[ignore] with a pointer to the accepted-state write-up, so the limitation is visible in the test output forever instead of quietly being papered over.

A caveat I want to be plain about: a great deal of the experimentation here was agent-assisted. The repository's commit log is unusually candid about it. The judgement calls are still mine, but I would not be able to run twenty-eight priced experiments in a week otherwise.

Where it stands

The project is at an acceptable release state as of September 27, and the autonomous search work is paused. Its own release notes put the two canonical tests at about 1.8 seconds each in a release test binary; on my machine a single recognize speech run takes about 0.4 s to load the corpus and 0.9 s to search.

The real progress: approximate mode works well, wreck a nice beach works, and a lot of general matching, scoring, runtime and diversity behaviour is measurably better than it was a week earlier.

The unresolved part, stated just as plainly: production search does not generate Hits Justice Dupe Hid Came. I checked the top 2,000 of a 20,522-candidate pool and the word hid does not appear in any of them, nor does dupe. The closest things on offer are it justice too pad same and it justice too pad came, which have the right bones and the wrong middle. That is a real limit on a real puzzle, and I would rather say so than make the readme look better than the program is.
